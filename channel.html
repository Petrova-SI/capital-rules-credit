02 / THE LENDING CHANNEL

From a capital rule to a loan decision.

A tighter requirement changes the value of capital inside the bank. The bank then decides whether to build its cushion, reprice risk or slow some forms of balance-sheet growth.

WHO MAY FEEL IT FIRST?

Borrowers do not all use the same amount of bank capital.

Risk weights and other prudential rules can make some exposures more capital-intensive than others. If a bank is constrained, it may prefer borrowers or products that use less scarce capital, have stronger collateral or offer a better risk-adjusted return.

That could affect small firms, unsecured consumer borrowers or higher-risk projects differently from low-risk, well-collateralised borrowers. The outcome depends on the specific rule and the bank’s portfolio; it should not be assumed in advance.

Price and quantity can move differently

A bank may keep lending but charge more for risk, tighten eligibility, shorten maturities or shift its portfolio. Lending activity is not one number.
