04 / THE DEBATE

Safer banks. At what cost?

A well-capitalised bank is better placed to absorb losses. Building a larger cushion can still affect the price and availability of credit, especially during a rapid adjustment.

POLICY TRADE-OFF≈ 4 MIN READ
+

THE CASE FOR BUFFERS

Resilience

  • More capacity to absorb unexpected losses
  • Lower chance that a shock forces a disorderly deleveraging
  • More room to keep serving clients during stress
↔

THE COST TO WATCH

Credit access

  • Capital may become more expensive or scarce
  • Some higher-risk or capital-intensive borrowers may face tighter terms
  • Rapid adjustment can weigh on investment and consumption

THE POLICY QUESTION

How fast should buffers rise?

Gradual increases can give banks time to retain earnings and plan capital. A buffer that can be released during a downturn may help banks absorb losses without cutting credit as sharply. The design, timing and release conditions all matter.

Regulators also monitor lending quality and borrower debt. Capital requirements are one tool among several, alongside monetary policy and borrower-based limits. Each addresses a different part of the risk.

A useful evaluation asks:

Did resilience improve? Which banks adjusted? Which borrowers faced changed terms? What else was happening to funding costs, interest rates and loan demand?

OUR TAKEAWAY

Capital rules can shape lending.
They do not determine it alone.

The effect is likely to depend on the bank’s starting position, its ability to build capital and the risks and returns of the loans it makes. That is why the mechanism is clear, while the measured impact needs careful evidence.
