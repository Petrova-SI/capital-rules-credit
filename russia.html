03 / RUSSIA IN FOCUS

Capital buffers meet fast credit growth.

The Bank of Russia introduced a positive national countercyclical capital buffer as credit expanded quickly and capital adequacy fell. The measures aim to strengthen resilience while supporting balanced lending.

WHAT THE NUMBERS DO NOT PROVE

Two things moved at once. That is not causation.

The 2024 figures show capital adequacy declining while loan portfolios grew quickly. They do not isolate the effect of capital rules from profits, interest rates, demand, risk weights, policy programmes or the wider economic environment.

The later recovery in the N1.0 ratio also coincided with profit reinvestment and slower lending growth. The Bank of Russia said these factors contributed to the increase. The figures are context for the mechanism, not a controlled estimate of how much lending changed because of one requirement.

Our interpretation

Capital rules can influence lending, especially when a bank is close to its constraint. Russian sector-wide data suggest the regulator has tried to rebuild buffers while monitoring credit growth. They do not show that tighter capital requirements alone caused lending to slow.

DATA NOTES

Percentages are from Bank of Russia publications. Corporate loan portfolio growth rates are nominal annual growth as reported by the regulator. The N1.0 figures are sector-level values and may differ by reporting date or bank coverage.
